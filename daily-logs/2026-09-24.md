## Notes

- Method: required prediction fields, discrete confidence, examples
- Issue: non significant latency change, few predictions accepted
- Additonally, first run had a runaway task that ignored cancellation, resulting in large latency. Patched by checking cancellation before and after every model call/tool execution and capping runaway task. Configured with `PREDICTIVE_AGENT4DRONE_MAX_WALL_SECONDS` and `PREDICTIVE_AGENT4DRONE_MAX_MODEL_CALLS`
- Method: Adaptive same-response one-step caching; prediction is optional on uncertain turns
- Issue: Preserved accuracy, but DeepSeek skipped prediction on most turns. Could not prove that prediction improved latency.
- Method: Selective mandatory prediction at coverage transitions, before typed-schema repair
- Issue: The gate incorrectly treated the word `polygon` as evidence of area coverage, so it activated for normal polygon-target and obstacle tasks. The planner described current actions as an unrestricted `list[dict]`, and DeepSeek generated empty inputs such as `take_off {}` and `move_along_path {}`. All four proposed predictions were rejected because their preceding current actions failed validation or execution.
- Method: Selective mandatory prediction after typed action schema and trigger repair
- Issue: Typed `tool` and `input` fields fixed the empty-argument failure, and two of three predictions were accepted. Prediction was still too sparse because only coverage transitions were mandatory and DeepSeek rarely used optional prediction elsewhere. One coverage prediction was rejected because `take_photo -> move_along_path` was not in the safe-transition allowlist even though the cached coverage-plan ID remained valid. Prediction-specific metrics now separate requested, proposed, accepted, and rejected predictions, but estimated milliseconds saved remain counterfactual rather than directly measured.

- Method: Concurrent background prediction alongside normal Agent4Drone planning (multi threading)
- Issue: The prediction used an additional LLM request. Fast UAV tools often completed before the prediction, causing timeouts or cancellation, while completed predictor calls increased latency, token usage, and total LLM calls.
- Method: Always-mandatory one-step prediction with planning limits
- Issue: Requiring prediction at every decision added schema and generation overhead even when the next action depended on new observations. Some tasks became slower or reached timeout/runaway limits. The limits stopped extreme runaway tasks, but several obstacle-heavy and flight-cycle tasks reached the 15-call budget before completing; most of those failures used no prediction and came from repeated obstacle-navigation attempts.
- The 11-task and 15-task runs use different task selections and should not be treated as a direct accuracy or latency comparison.
- The latest 15-task run proposed only three predictions. Two were accepted and estimated to save two LLM calls and 8.396 seconds, but this was too little prediction coverage to materially affect the full-run latency.
- The 15 tasks were the first of each category + difficulty level. This ensures short check times while covering a wide range of task types.

## Next Steps

- Replace the coverage-keyword gate with a transition-based gate for routine deterministic sequences
- Require lookahead for safe transitions such as takeoff to altitude change, takeoff to rotation, successful navigation to hover/photo, hover to photo/return-home, and return-home to landing
- Keep obstacle-heavy, failed-movement, and unresolved-sensing transitions on normal step-by-step Agent4Drone planning
- Allow safe coverage sequences such as `generate_coverage_path -> take_photo -> move_along_path` when the coverage-plan ID is still valid
- Add prediction-opportunity and proposal-rate metrics so low model participation can be distinguished from a narrow gate
- Rerun predictive and normal Agent4Drone on the same fixed task set with multiple trials before claiming a latency improvement
